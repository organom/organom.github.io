This is a continuation of the [secretlab titan chair renewal](/2026-02-21-secretlab-titan-renewal/). After swapping in the new [Secretlab TITAN](https://secretlab.co/pages/titan-evo-2022-series) upholstery, I was left holding the old backrest and seat pans. The foam and shells were perfectly fine — only the leatherette had failed — and throwing them out felt wrong.

Then I remembered Manuel, an older friend of mine who has spent a lifetime as an auto upholsterer. If anyone could give those parts a second life, it was him. I'd end up with a customized chair made by someone I like, and one I'd sit in every single day.

## the brief

I handed him the parts with only two requirements: **dark colour**, and **I need to sit on it 12h+ a day**. Everything else was his call — that's the whole point of asking someone who knows what they're doing.

He came back with chevron quilting and contrast stitching, in automotive-grade material. Proper car-seat construction, not gaming-chair construction.

## the second chair idea

Once the reupholstered parts were back, another idea took shape. If I ordered the *remaining* pieces from Secretlab — base, casters, gas lift, armrests, side covers — the old shells would become a complete second chair for the office. Two chairs to compare side by side, and a full set of spares if anything ever fails on either one.

Secretlab's [spare parts store](https://secretlab.co/products/spare-parts) covers most of it, and their [support team](https://secretlab.co/pages/support) handles what isn't listed publicly. Collecting everything took a few months of ordering in batches.

The pistons were the only two parts I didn't get from Secretlab — those came from Amazon. Took the opportunity to replace the main piston on the first chair too, while I was at it. I ordered the wrong outside fitting design on that first one, so it needed a bit of persuading to sit properly in the original shell. Didn't repeat that mistake on the second piston — check the fitting profile, not just the length and class.

## lowering the armrests

While everything was apart, I also fixed a long-standing annoyance: the armrests hitting the desk. This [Secretlab chair armrest spacer](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1251666-secretlab-chair-armrest-spacer) drops them another 25mm, low enough to clear the desk without fouling the adjustment controls. You need 3x M8x50mm bolts and washers per arm to fit them.

## the result

Side by side, the custom one holds up. The chevron pattern is a breathable fabric, with leatherette kept to the sides and bolsters only. It doesn't run substantially cooler, but it does breathe more, and it grips — you slide around far less than on an all-leatherette seat, so you end up sitting more stable overall. That turned out to matter more than I expected.

It costs more and takes more work than simply binning the old parts. But I ended up with two working chairs instead of one, a full set of replacement parts should I ever need them, and a chair customized by a friend. Definitely worth it.
